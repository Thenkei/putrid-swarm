# Lore bible and narrative handoff

## Canon boundary

The original project's lore documents were not accessible. This bible records the narrative facts in the shared chat and supplies a clearly marked provisional treatment. It does not replace recovered lore or claim to reproduce it.

**Established:** the main character begins at a desk; the welcome screen is their computer screen. A joint sits in an ashtray to the right. News channels report the arrival of zombies and mention patient zero. The character takes a large drag; smoke obscures the image, and a time jump brings the player into the adventure. The adventure includes the house, old car, neighborhood, infected animals and contaminated neighbors as bosses.

**Open:** the character's name, background, gender identity, town, country, date, infection cause, patient-zero identity, infection rules, elapsed time and ultimate objective. The smoke is an editing transition; the chat does not establish a dream, hallucination, immunity or cannabis-based protection.

## Provisional narrative treatment — proposed

The neighborhood is familiar enough that people are first recognized by routine: a mower at the same hour, deliveries arriving at the same doors, a mechanic still keeping track of bills. When the outbreak reaches it, those details become warning signs. The house remains the player's fixed point. Its repairs and improvised surveillance show how an ordinary room has become a place to survive the night.

During the day, the protagonist takes the old car through streets whose small changes reveal what happened: unfinished work, abandoned equipment, missing residents. Resources brought home become defenses the player personally places. At night, the camera feeds reveal the same spaces under attack, including familiar people whose habits now determine how they threaten the house.

The narrative can advance through encounters and environmental changes between cycles. This avoids requiring a large exposition sequence before the central loop works. No origin conspiracy, faction hierarchy or cure is proposed as canon.

### Placeholder broadcast — newly written, provisional

> Reports of violent incidents are now coming from several neighborhoods. Authorities refer to an initial case as “patient zero,” but have not confirmed the source of the outbreak. Residents are being advised to remain indoors while emergency services assess the situation.

Use an original fictional broadcaster and original wording. Reporting patient zero is not proof that the broadcaster knows the true origin. This text is a starting point for writing, not scientific exposition or recovered dialogue.

## Neighbors before and after

The creator wants uninfected and infected neighbors, with bosses becoming increasingly important local figures. Meeting them alive during outings is an assistant proposal; infection need not happen as a live simulation. The names below remain role labels.

| Character | Before infection — source suggestion | Infected identity — source suggestion | Cocasse detail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mower neighbor | Protective of their immaculate lawn; complains about the car intruding | Charges straight and breaks barricades | Still cuts perfectly straight strips |
| Mechanic | Recognizable through workshop equipment and billing | Uses equipment as a ram | The bill stays attached |
| Mail carrier | Continues a familiar delivery round | Moves fast and exploits breaches | Still determined to finish the route |
| Police chief | Local authority figure | Protects and directs a group | Still tries to maintain order |
| Mayor | Public figure campaigning locally | Calls reinforcements with a megaphone | Still campaigning |

**Proposed character production rule:** establish one everyday habit, one silhouette/prop and one sound while a character is alive; preserve those identifiers after infection. Each boss should change the defense problem, rather than merely increase health.

The mayor and police chief are creator examples, not confirmed named antagonists. Sheriff is an alternative role conditional on choosing a US setting; do not include both automatically.

## Tone and recurring details

The creator asks for cocasse humor. The assistant suggests that infected people remain convinced they are doing ordinary things. Play the danger seriously and let the absurdity appear in behavior: a perfectly mown strip through a devastated garden, a bill on a makeshift ram, a political slogan interrupting a night attack.

The assistant mentioned *Shaun of the Dead* as a tonal reference for banal daily life alongside horror. Treat this as a reference, not permission to copy characters, dialogue, visual identity or plot.

**Proposed writing constraints:** avoid constant punchlines and an omniscient explanation of the infection. Give the house and car continuity through visible wear. Keep any actual infection mechanics consistent once chosen; retained habits are a creative motif, not yet a biological rule.

## Narrative questions for the creator

- What from the original lore must survive, and can its documents be recovered?
- Where does the story take place, and what makes this neighborhood distinctive?
- Why does the protagonist remain at this house? What are they trying to achieve beyond the next night?
- How long does the opening ellipsis last, and what changed during it?
- When and how do living neighbors become bosses? Is that fixed, preventable or unknown to the player?
- Is patient zero only a news hook, a mystery to investigate, or an eventual encounter?
- What constitutes a completed story or run?

These questions do not block a mechanical prototype. They do block presenting a final campaign synopsis as settled lore.
